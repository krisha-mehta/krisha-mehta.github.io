    <div class="about-text">
        <p>
            Hi! I am Krisha Mehta, a PhD student in the <a href="https://cs.uchicago.edu/" target="_blank">Department of Computer Science</a> at the <a href="https://www.uchicago.edu/" target="_blank">University of Chicago</a>. I am advised by Prof. <a href="https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~kalea/" target="_blank">Alex Kale</a> at the <a href="https://data-cognition-lab.org/" target="_blank">Data Cognition Lab</a>.
        </p>

                    <div class="pub-abstract">
                        <!-- Add your abstract here -->
                        Data visualizations often communicate more than their designers intend—revealing sensitive information about individuals or groups in ways that may undermine privacy. This paper examines how visualization designers can intentionally reason about what information is disclosed, to whom, and under what conditions. We present a framework for designing for disclosure, drawing on concepts from privacy theory and data ethics, and illustrate its application through a set of annotated design examples.
